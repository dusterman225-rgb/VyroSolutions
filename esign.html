Some Vyqil features let you sign documents electronically (for example, accepting an estimate). This page explains what that means, as described in the U.S. federal E-SIGN Act and similar state laws (UETA).

Your agreement to do business electronically

When you tick the box that says you agree to use electronic records and signatures, you agree that: (1) you may sign using your typed name, a drawn signature and a click, and these count as your legal signature; (2) records, notices and documents related to that transaction may be given to you electronically; and (3) a signed electronic document has the same effect as a paper one, to the extent the law allows.

How a signature is recorded

  • The name you typed and, if you drew one, your signature image.
  • The date and time (set by our server), your time zone and a description of your device.
  • A digital fingerprint of the exact document content you signed, so any later change can be detected.
  • Your agreement to this disclosure.

Your rights

  • Paper copy: you may ask the business that sent the document for a paper copy at any time. A fee may apply as that business decides.
  • Withdraw consent: you may withdraw your agreement to use electronic records before you sign by not signing, and ask the business to deal with you on paper. Withdrawing does not affect documents you already signed.
  • Update your contact details: tell the business and update your email in your Vyqil account.
  • Copies: you should keep or download a copy. The business that sent the document keeps its signed record in Vyqil.

What you need

A current web browser, an internet connection, and an email address you can access. You need a way to view and save PDF or web pages.

Important limits

  • Vyqil provides the tool. It is not a party to your agreement and cannot say whether a particular document is valid or enforceable.
  • Some documents cannot be signed electronically or need extra steps, for example wills and trusts, certain court and family-law papers, some notices (such as certain utility, eviction or foreclosure notices), and some real-estate or notarized documents. Check with your attorney.
  • Only sign if you are the person named, or are authorized to sign for them. Signing for someone else without permission may be unlawful.
  • Read the full document before you sign.
